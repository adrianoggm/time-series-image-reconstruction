\begin{abstract}
% <= 200 words.
Image representations of time series are increasingly used to generate or restore signals, which requires mapping an image back to a series. We establish a common framework to evaluate how much of a series established representations retain: the recurrence plot (RP; binary, unsigned and signed), the Gramian angular summation and difference fields (GASF, GADF), the Markov transition field (MTF) and XIRP. Every image is stored with 8 bits and decoded from the image alone, with published inverses where they exist and with decoders we propose where none applied: a geometric decoder for the signed RP, with sufficient conditions for exact recovery, and an anchored decoder for GADF. On seven datasets from six domains we measure fidelity, robustness, cost and downstream classification accuracy. The signed RP, GASF, GADF on a restricted angular range and XIRP were recovered to machine precision in floating point on all but one window and, after 8-bit storage, lost at most \clsMaxDropExact{} of accuracy on the labelled datasets. The signed RP degrades as its states become collinear, as at high sampling rates. The unsigned RP is recovered only up to sign; with the evaluated decoders, the binary RP and MTF lose most of the waveform.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

Recurrence plots (RP), Gramian angular fields (GAF) and Markov transition fields (MTF) turn a time series into a matrix that relates every pair of instants, so that image models can learn from temporal data~\cite{wang2015encoding,wang2015encoding2,eckmann1987recurrence,marwan2007recurrence}. The same representations are now used in the opposite direction: a generative or restoration model works on images, and its output has to be turned back into a series~\cite{huang2023tsdigan,hellermann2026imagegan}. A classifier can succeed on an image that has discarded amplitude, phase or temporal order; a generator is useful as a signal model only if its images can be decoded into the signals they stand for. Reconstruction has nevertheless been studied one representation at a time, usually on one kind of signal, and often only through the accuracy of a downstream classifier.

This study establishes a common framework for evaluating the reconstruction capacity of established image representations of time series that are built from relations between pairs of instants; time--frequency and time--scale images, such as spectrograms and scalograms, are outside its scope. It does not seek the best representation for reconstruction: a model may need to work with a particular image for the structure it exposes, such as the recurrences of an RP, and then needs to know whether and how a series can be read back from it, with what error and under which conditions. We study the RP, the Gramian angular summation and difference fields (GASF, GADF) and the MTF, together with the extended intertemporal return plot (XIRP)~\cite{hellermann2026imagegan}, which stores the series on its diagonal and serves as a reference that is invertible by construction. For the RP we separate the encoding, binary, continuous unsigned or signed~\cite{lu2019robust}, from the decoder: the reconstructions of Hirata et al.~\cite{hirata2008reproduction} and Sipers et al.~\cite{sipers2017robust}, or a geometric decoder. Every image is stored with 8 bits per pixel and decoded from the image alone (Figure~\ref{fig:workflow}). We address four questions:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{RQ1, capability.} What can each representation recover from the image alone, in time and frequency, and how much of the loss is due to 8-bit storage?
\item \emph{RQ2, methods.} How far do the proposed decoders extend the available procedures, when are they exact, and what do encoding and decoder each contribute for recurrence plots?
\item \emph{RQ3, fidelity and robustness.} How do quantisation, signal characteristics such as the sampling rate, and image perturbations affect reconstruction?
\item \emph{RQ4, applicability.} What does reconstruction cost, and do reconstructions keep what classifiers trained on original signals need?
\end{itemize}

The contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item A common, reproducible framework: ten encoding--decoder combinations on seven public datasets from six domains and three window lengths, with one protocol of fidelity, robustness, cost and classification utility that separates representation loss from storage loss.
\item Decoders for representations without a suitable image-only inverse: building on reconstruction from binary and unthresholded RPs, a geometric decoder for the signed RP (multidimensional scaling, adjacent-state overlap and sign-based orientation) with sufficient conditions for exact recovery, an anchored decoder for GADF on a restricted angular range, and an approximate representative-trajectory decoder for MTF.
\item A basis for image-based generation of time series: what each image retains, how its reconstruction degrades after storage and under perturbation, which conditions it needs, including the conditioning of the signed-RP states, and what it costs.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

Recurrence plots show when a dynamical system revisits a region of its state space~\cite{eckmann1987recurrence,marwan2007recurrence}. The conventional RP is binary; continuous variants keep the distance between states, and the signed variant of Lu and Tong also keeps the direction of the displacement~\cite{lu2019robust}. Wang and Oates proposed GASF, GADF and MTF as inputs to convolutional networks~\cite{wang2015encoding,wang2015encoding2}, and these representations have been applied to activity recognition~\cite{ahmad2021inertial}, anomaly detection~\cite{ma2024anomaly} and imputation~\cite{huamin2020reconstruction}. XIRP was introduced for time-series generation with image-based generative adversarial networks, with the explicit aim of making generated images invertible~\cite{hellermann2026imagegan}.

Inversion has been studied separately for each representation. For binary RPs, Hirata et al.\ approximate distances between states by shortest paths in a recurrence graph and recover the states by multidimensional scaling~\cite{hirata2008reproduction,hirata2016chromosome}. For unthresholded RPs of delay embeddings, Sipers et al.\ give spectral conditions under which the RP determines the signal up to its mean and sign, and a reconstruction algorithm tested under disturbances and on multi-level RPs~\cite{sipers2011unique,sipers2017robust}. Both lines of work take an unsigned input, thresholded or not, so the sign of the series remains undetermined; neither uses the directional sign of the signed RP. GASF is read from its diagonal, after restoring damaged images with denoising autoencoders~\cite{wang2015encoding,huamin2020reconstruction}; approximate recovery from MTF has been reported to be difficult~\cite{wang2015encoding}; XIRP is inverted from its diagonal or by averaging the proposals of all its columns~\cite{hellermann2026imagegan}. We are not aware of a decoder that uses the sign of the signed RP to fix the orientation from a stored image, of a GADF decoder that resolves its branch ambiguity and its lost angular offset from the image alone, or of a comparison of these representations under a common protocol that separates representation loss from storage loss or, for recurrence plots, the contribution of the encoding from that of the decoder.

\section{Mathematical formulation}\label{sec:formulation}

\subsection{Encoding, storage and decoding}
Let $x=(x_1,\ldots,x_T)$ be one channel of a window. Every method $m$ applies a calibration $\mathcal N$ to a method-specific normalised domain, an encoder $\mathcal E_m$, a fixed 8-bit storage map $\mathcal Q_m$ and a decoder $\mathcal D_m$:
\[
\hat x = \mathcal N^{-1}\bigl(\mathcal D_m(\mathcal Q_m(\mathcal E_m(\mathcal N(x))));\,x_{\max},x_{\min}\bigr).
\]
The calibration $(x_{\max},x_{\min})$ is shared by all methods and is the only per-channel information used to return to physical units. We keep three properties apart: \emph{identifiability}, whether $\mathcal E_m$ is injective on the normalised domain, possibly up to a stated symmetry; \emph{exactness} of a given decoder, whether $\mathcal D_m\circ\mathcal E_m$ is the identity under stated hypotheses, so that all error is due to $\mathcal Q_m$; and \emph{stability}, how much the error grows after $\mathcal Q_m$ or other perturbations of the image. A representation is \emph{many-to-one} when distinct normalised series share the same matrix; no decoder can then be exact for all series.

\subsection{Recurrence plots}
The three RP encodings are built on the adjacent states
\[
s_i=(x_i,x_{i+1})\in\mathbb R^2,\qquad i=1,\ldots,T-1,
\]
a two-dimensional delay embedding with unit delay~\cite{takens1981detecting}, and differ in what they keep of the displacements $s_i-s_j$. The \emph{binary} RP keeps only whether two states are close~\cite{eckmann1987recurrence,marwan2007recurrence}, $B_{ij}=\mathbb 1[\lVert s_i-s_j\rVert_2<\varepsilon]$; it is many-to-one, and any reconstruction from it has to approximate distances from neighbourhoods. The \emph{unsigned} RP, also called an unthresholded or global RP~\cite{marwan2007recurrence,sipers2011unique}, keeps the distances $D_{ij}=\lVert s_i-s_j\rVert_2$. As a Euclidean distance matrix it determines the states up to a translation and an orthogonal transformation of the plane, and the series $x$ and its reflection $-x$ have the same $D$, so it is at best identifiable up to sign. It is stored as $D/\max_{ij}D_{ij}$.

The \emph{signed} RP of Lu and Tong~\cite{lu2019robust} adds whether each displacement follows a reference direction. With $v=(1,1)/\sqrt2$ and $\hat u_{ij}=(s_i-s_j)/\lVert s_i-s_j\rVert_2$,
\begin{equation}
R_{ij}=\sigma_{ij}\lVert s_i-s_j\rVert_2,\qquad
\sigma_{ij}=\begin{cases}+1,& \lVert s_i-s_j\rVert_2=0\ \text{or}\ \hat u_{ij}\cdot v\ge\cos(\pi/4),\\-1,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}
\label{eq:signed_rp}
\end{equation}
Its magnitude is the unsigned RP, $|R|=D$. A pair is \emph{sign-informative} when its displacement lies within $\pi/4$ of $\pm v$; then $\sigma_{ij}=-\sigma_{ji}$, while displacements closer to the anti-diagonal receive $-1$ in both orders. The convention that marks only displacements within $\pi/4$ of $-v$ differs only on non-informative pairs and carries the same information. The signed RP is stored with a per-window affine map; its zero diagonal marks the level of $R=0$ in the image (Section~\ref{sec:storage}).

Exact recovery needs a recoverable geometry, an overlap that leaves only $x$ and $-x$, and a sign that tells them apart. The first two concern the unsigned geometry, studied by Sipers et al.\ for delay embeddings~\cite{sipers2011unique}; the third is what the sign adds.

\begin{proposition}[Signed RP on exact input]\label{prop:rp}
Let $S\in\mathbb R^{(T-1)\times2}$ have rows $s_i$, and assume
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] the centred states span $\mathbb R^2$, i.e.\ $\sigma_2(S-\bar S)>0$;
\item[(b)] for $i=1,\ldots,T-2$, $x$ satisfies neither \emph{(R)} $x_i+x_{i+2}=\kappa$ nor \emph{(F)} $\alpha(x_{i+2}-x_i)=\beta x_{i+1}+\kappa$, for any constants $\kappa$ and $(\alpha,\beta)\neq(0,0)$;
\item[(c)] at least one pair of states is sign-informative.
\end{enumerate}
Then the decoder of Section~\ref{sec:rp_decoder} applied to the floating-point signed RP returns $x$ on the normalised domain. Without (c), $x$ and $-x$ have the same signed RP, so no decoder that uses the image alone can tell them apart. Under (a), condition (b) is equivalent to one of the matrices $H_\eta$ of step~3 having a simple zero eigenvalue and the other being positive definite, so it can be checked from the image; it holds, in particular, whenever the centred triples $(x_i,x_{i+1},x_{i+2})$, $i=1,\ldots,T-2$, have rank three.
\end{proposition}
\noindent\emph{Proof.} By (a), classical MDS applied to $|R|$ returns $Y$ with $Y=SP_0+\mathbf 1t^\top$ for some $P_0\in O(2)$~\cite{Torgerson1952}, so every candidate configuration is $Z=SP+\mathbf 1c^\top$ with $P\in O(2)$. Writing $(a_i,b_i)$ for the rows of $Z$, the residual $b_i-a_{i+1}$ is constant in $i$ if and only if $P_{12}x_i+(P_{22}-P_{11})x_{i+1}-P_{21}x_{i+2}$ is. For a rotation, $P=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}c&s\\-s&c\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$, this reads $s(x_i+x_{i+2})=\mathrm{const}$; for a reflection, $P=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}c&s\\s&-c\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$, it reads $s(x_i-x_{i+2})-2c\,x_{i+1}=\mathrm{const}$. Under (b) the only solutions are $s=0$ with $P$ a rotation, i.e.\ $P=\pm I$, so the overlap is satisfied exactly by the states of $x$ and $-x$ alone, and one of the two matrices $H_\eta$ of step~3 has a simple zero eigenvalue, whose eigenvector, taken with both signs, yields these two candidates. Conversely, (R) makes every rotation a solution, so one $H_\eta$ vanishes, and (F) gives a reflection solution, a zero eigenvalue of the other parity; this proves the equivalence. In step~4, $x$ reproduces $R$ exactly. Any candidate that also reproduces $R$, up to the positive scale removed by the normalisation, has states with distance matrix proportional to $|R|$ that satisfy the overlap by construction, and is therefore $\pm x$ up to scale and translation, which the final range map removes. Reflecting the series maps $s_i-s_j$ to $s_j-s_i$, so $\sigma_{ij}$ becomes $\sigma_{ji}$: on a sign-informative pair the entry changes sign, on a non-informative pair it does not. Hence the signed RPs of $x$ and $-x$ differ if and only if (c) holds, and under (c) the selected candidate is $x$. $\square$

Neither (b) nor (c) follows from (a). A sinusoid at a quarter of the sampling rate, $x_i=\sin(\pi i/2+\phi)$, satisfies (R): every rotation then meets the overlap, and the phases $\phi=0.2$ and $\phi=0.25$ give the same signed RP, so identifiability can be lost altogether. Condition (b) is sufficient, not necessary: under (F) the additional reflection can still be discarded by the sign. The series $x=(1,-1.05,1.10,-1.15,1.20,-1.25,1.30,-1.35)$ satisfies (a) and (b), but every displacement lies close to the anti-diagonal, so no pair is informative and $x$ and $-x$ have the same signed RP. In the benchmark, all \signSeries{} channel series have a sign-informative pair, with a smallest share of \signMinShare\%, and the floating-point decoder was exact on all but one degenerate series (Section~\ref{sec:results_fidelity}), so neither failure affected the results.

\begin{remark}[Stability after 8-bit storage]\label{rem:conditioning}
Proposition~\ref{prop:rp} concerns exact input. Storage perturbs $|R|$ by at most half a quantisation step relative to its range; by the Davis--Kahan theorem, the resulting error of the recovered two-dimensional subspace is bounded by the perturbation divided by the gap between the second and third eigenvalues of the centred Gram matrix~\cite{davis1970rotation}, which shrinks as $\sigma_2/\sigma_1$ of the centred states tends to zero. We therefore use $\sigma_2/\sigma_1$ as an indicator of conditioning. It does not cover errors in the choice of orientation, which depend on the overlap residual and on the sign comparison.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Gramian angular fields}
The Gramian angular fields~\cite{wang2015encoding} map each normalised sample to an angle, $\phi_i=\arccos\tilde x_i$, and store
\[
G^{\mathrm S}_{ij}=\cos(\phi_i+\phi_j),\qquad G^{\mathrm D}_{ij}=\sin(\phi_i-\phi_j).
\]
The diagonal of GASF, $\cos(2\phi_i)$, determines $\tilde x_i$ only up to sign on $[-1,1]$, but uniquely on $[0,1]$, which we use: there $\phi_i\in[0,\pi/2]$ and $\tilde x_i=\cos\bigl(\tfrac12\arccos G^{\mathrm S}_{ii}\bigr)$, so GASF is exactly invertible from its diagonal.

GADF is antisymmetric with a zero diagonal: it stores only relative angles. Inverting it requires the principal branch of $\arcsin$ to return $\phi_i-\phi_j$ and the lost global offset to be restored. If each window is scaled to the symmetric interval $[-\alpha,\alpha]$,
\[
\max_{i,j}|\phi_i-\phi_j|=\pi-2\arccos\alpha<\frac\pi2\iff\alpha<\frac1{\sqrt2},
\]
so with $\alpha=0.7$ no entry leaves the principal branch, and since every non-constant window reaches both bounds $\pm\alpha$ after scaling, the offset can be fixed from the image itself (Section~\ref{sec:gadf_decoder}). Exactness therefore holds for this restricted, known normalisation; it is not a property of GADF in general, and images encoded on a wider angular range keep the branch ambiguity.

\subsection{Markov transition fields}
MTF assigns every sample to one of $Q$ quantile states $q_t$, estimates the transition matrix $W_{pq}=P(q_{t+1}=q\mid q_t=p)$ and sets $M_{ab}=W_{q_aq_b}$~\cite{wang2015encoding}. Samples in the same state are indistinguishable, the image values do not fix the numerical order of the states, and different series with the same state sequence produce the same image. MTF is therefore many-to-one: no decoder can return every series exactly, and decoding means choosing a representative trajectory.

\subsection{Extended intertemporal return plot}
XIRP~\cite{hellermann2026imagegan} is defined for a strictly positive series as $R_{ij}=\log(x_i/x_j)$ for $i\ne j$ and $R_{ii}=x_i$. Its diagonal is the series, so it is exactly invertible by construction; every column $j$ also proposes $x_i=x_j\exp(R_{ij})$, a redundancy that inversion by mean averages. Positivity is obtained by mapping each channel to $[1,2]$ with the shared calibration.

\section{Decoders}\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Storage protocol}\label{sec:storage}
Each matrix is written to $[0,255]$ by a fixed or per-window affine map. GASF and GADF are read back with the fixed range $[-1,1]$, MTF with $[0,1]$, and XIRP with the fixed ranges of its diagonal, $[1,2]$, and off-diagonal, $[-\log2,\log2]$. The signed RP keeps a per-window map; because its diagonal is zero, subtracting the median diagonal pixel recovers the signed matrix up to a positive scale, which MDS and the final range map absorb. No decoder uses per-window metadata.

\subsection{Geometric decoder on the signed RP}\label{sec:rp_decoder}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Recover $R^{\mathrm{obs}}=\mathrm{PNG}-\operatorname{median}_i\mathrm{PNG}_{ii}$ and $D_{ij}=\tfrac12(|R^{\mathrm{obs}}_{ij}|+|R^{\mathrm{obs}}_{ji}|)$.
\item Classical MDS~\cite{Torgerson1952}: with $J=I-\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^\top/(T-1)$, the two leading eigenpairs of $-\tfrac12JD^{\circ2}J$ give $Y=V_2\Lambda_2^{1/2}$.
\item Orientation in closed form. Write $Q=\mathrm{rot}(\theta)\operatorname{diag}(1,\eta)$, $\eta=\pm1$, and $(a_i,b_i)$ for the rows of $YQ$. For fixed $\eta$ the overlap residual is $b_i-a_{i+1}=A_i\cos\theta+B_i\sin\theta$, with $A_i=\eta Y_{i2}-Y_{i+1,1}$ and $B_i=-\eta Y_{i1}-Y_{i+1,2}$. Profiling out a common translation centres $A$ and $B$, and the squared residual becomes the Rayleigh quotient $u^\top H_\eta u$, $H_\eta=\tilde C^\top\tilde C$, $\tilde C=[\tilde A\ \tilde B]$, $u=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)$. The eigenvector of the smaller eigenvalue of each $2\times2$ matrix $H_\eta$, with both signs, gives four candidates.
\item Sign selection. Keep the candidate whose regenerated signed RP is closest to $R^{\mathrm{obs}}$ in Frobenius norm, both normalised to unit norm.
\item Samples. Correct the translation, $a_i\mapsto a_i-\delta^\star/2$ and $b_i\mapsto b_i+\delta^\star/2$ with $\delta^\star=-\operatorname{mean}_i(b_i-a_{i+1})$; set $\hat x_1=a_1$, $\hat x_i=\tfrac12(b_{i-1}+a_i)$ for $2\le i\le T-1$ and $\hat x_T=b_{T-1}$, and map their range to the normalised domain.
\end{enumerate}
Orientation costs $O(T)$; the decoder is dominated by the eigendecomposition, $O(T^3)$ in a dense implementation.

\subsection{Decoders that isolate the sign and the decoder}\label{sec:hirata}
\emph{Geometric decoder on the unsigned RP.} The distances are read directly from the image. Steps~2, 3 and~5 are applied unchanged; step~4 is not available, and the candidate with the smallest overlap residual is kept. On exact distances $x$ and $-x$ fit the overlap equally well, so which one is returned is arbitrary.

\emph{Hirata's reconstruction.} As the reference for binary RPs we implement Hirata et al.~\cite{hirata2008reproduction,hirata2016chromosome}. The unsigned image is thresholded into a binary RP $B$; an edge joins $i\ne j$ when $B_{ij}=1$, with length $1-|G_i\cap G_j|/|G_i\cup G_j|$ and $G_i=\{k:B_{ik}=1\}$; all-pairs shortest paths give distances and classical MDS gives the states. Turning the states into $T$ samples is our post-processing: the leading MDS coordinate, the mean appended to reach $T$ samples, and the range map. The method does not prescribe the threshold, and its authors report that it still works with up to 70\% of the map set to one~\cite{hirata2016chromosome}; we fixed a recurrence rate of $0.75$ in advance and checked it against rates from $0.02$ to $0.75$ (Section~\ref{sec:results_rp}). On the signed RP the graph is built from $|R|$, so the sign cannot change the recovered geometry; it is used, as in step~4, to choose between the result and its reflection.

\emph{Sipers' reconstruction.} For unthresholded RPs we also implement the method of Sipers et al.~\cite{sipers2017robust}: the two-dimensional Fourier coefficients of the squared RP factor into products of the signal's Fourier coefficients, recovered up to a sign by an eigendecomposition. It assumes a periodic signal and a circular embedding, so it decodes the circular variant of the RP, with the wrap state $(x_T,x_1)$ added and the same 8-bit maps. Frequencies whose power, estimated from the image, is below $10^{-4}$ of the largest are discarded; this global threshold minimised its own 8-bit error in a pilot, which favours the method (Supplementary Section~S5). On the signed RP, the sign selects between the result and its reflection.

Hirata's method thus receives only the binary information left by thresholding, whereas the geometric decoder uses continuous distances. To separate the loss caused by thresholding from that of the reconstruction algorithm, both decoders are also run on the floating-point binary RP with the orientation chosen by an oracle that knows the original; the geometric decoder then uses the shortest-path distances of the binary graph.

\subsection{GADF decoder}\label{sec:gadf_decoder}
GADF on $[-\alpha,\alpha]$, $\alpha=0.7$, is decoded in three steps.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Boundary anchor.} The row means are $\mu_i=\frac1T\sum_jG^{\mathrm D}_{ij}=\rho\sin(\phi_i-\psi)$, where $\rho\ge0$ and $\psi$ lies inside the cone spanned by the sample angles. That cone is narrower than $\pi/2$, so $\mu$ is strictly increasing over it, $e=\arg\max_i|\mu_i|$ is a boundary sample, and $\phi_e=\arccos(-\alpha)$ if $\mu_e>0$ and $\arccos\alpha$ otherwise.
\item \emph{Relative angles.} For a pivot row $p$, $\hat\phi^{(p)}_j=\phi_e-\arcsin G^{\mathrm D}_{ep}-\arcsin G^{\mathrm D}_{pj}$.
\item \emph{Pivot averaging.} The angles from the five rows with the smallest $|\mu_p|$ are averaged, $\hat{\tilde x}_j=\cos\bigl(\frac15\sum_p\hat\phi^{(p)}_j\bigr)$, which reduces the effect of storage.
\end{enumerate}
The decoder is exact in real arithmetic under the hypotheses of Section~\ref{sec:formulation} and costs $O(T^2)$.

\subsection{GASF, MTF and XIRP}
GASF is decoded with the diagonal inverse of Wang and Oates~\cite{wang2015encoding}. For MTF, the $T$ rows are clustered into $Q=4$ groups, since rows of the same state are equal in a clean MTF; the transition matrix between groups is estimated from the image blocks, the groups are ordered by the Fiedler vector of the Laplacian of its symmetrisation, following spectral seriation~\cite{Atkins1998}, and each sample in ordered group $k$ is assigned the bin midpoint $(k-\tfrac12)/Q$. The global direction of that order is not determined by the image, and the error of this decoder combines what MTF discards with its own choices; the two are not separated here. XIRP is decoded with the inversion by mean of Hellermann and Lessmann~\cite{hellermann2026imagegan}.

\section{Experimental protocol}\label{sec:protocol}

\subsection{Datasets and windows}
Table~\ref{tab:datasets} lists the seven public datasets: human activity (WISDM~\cite{misc_wisdm_smartphone_and_smartwatch_activity_and_biometrics_dataset__507}, PAMAP2~\cite{reiss2012pamap2}), environmental audio (ESC-50~\cite{piczak2015esc}), electrocardiography (PTB-XL~\cite{wagner2020ptbxl}), geomagnetism (Swarm~\cite{friis2006swarm}), seismology (STEAD~\cite{mousavi2019stead}) and surface electromyography (EMG~\cite{krilova2018emg}). Every channel is encoded, stored and decoded as its own single-channel image with its own calibration. Recordings are cut into non-overlapping windows of $T\in\{64,128,256\}$ samples that never cross a label change or a time gap. Blocks with fewer than 200 windows (EMG at $T=256$) are not reported.

\subsection{Fidelity, robustness and cost}
Fidelity uses 500 windows per dataset and length drawn with a fixed seed. Writing $r=\max_ix_i-\min_ix_i$, it is measured per channel series by
\[
\mathrm{NMAE}=\frac{1}{rT}\sum_i|x_i-\hat x_i|,
\]
the Pearson correlation, the $L_1$ distance between unit-mass Welch spectra~\cite{Welch1967} and the relative energy error; constant series are excluded. Each decoder is run on the floating-point matrix and on the 8-bit image. Robustness adds zero-mean Gaussian noise of $\sigma\in\{2,4,8,16\}$ intensity levels to every pixel of the stored image, which is then clipped and rounded, with two replicates per level on 200 windows per dataset and length. Cost is the single-thread time to encode, store and decode and the peak traced memory, for $T$ from 64 to 1\,024. The sampling-rate study reads 3\,000 PTB-XL records at 500\,Hz and resamples them with an anti-aliased polyphase filter to 250, 100 and 50\,Hz, with windows of 128 samples and, as a control, of a fixed duration of 1.28\,s.

\subsection{Classification utility}
Classifiers are trained on original windows and evaluated on the original and on every reconstruction of the same held-out windows. Up to 6\,000 windows per dataset and length are split into three grouped folds that keep every subject, patient, event, station or source recording on one side, stratified by class for single-label data; per-channel standardisation uses training statistics only, and reconstructions are produced independently per window, so no information crosses the split. KNN, an RBF SVM, a random forest, an LSTM, a GRU and a one-dimensional CNN are used with standard settings (Supplementary Section~S1). Accuracy, or macro-F1 for the multilabel PTB-XL, is averaged over the three folds. Swarm has no labels. ESC-50 windows of 128 samples span 16\,ms of sound, which leaves every classifier near chance, so audio is evaluated on the official ESC-10 subset at 2, 4 and 8\,kHz with windows of 512 and 1\,024 samples, silent windows removed and accuracy scored per clip.

\subsection{Statistical protocol}
All comparisons are paired over the same series; differences between decoders are summarised by medians of per-series differences, averaged across datasets with equal weight. A reconstruction is declared \emph{equivalent} to the original for a classifier when the $95\%$ bootstrap interval of the accuracy difference, resampling whole groups~\cite{DavisonHinkley1997,Hall1987}, lies within $\pm0.01$, which corresponds to two one-sided tests at the $2.5\%$ level~\cite{Schuirmann1987,Lakens2017}; a non-significant difference is not taken as evidence of equivalence. Equivalence is assessed on single-label data; for PTB-XL only the drop in macro-F1 is reported.

\subsection{Fidelity and storage (RQ1)}\label{sec:results_fidelity}
Table~\ref{tab:fidelity} gives the 8-bit error of every combination at $T=128$; the other lengths behave alike (Supplementary Tables~S1--S2). Over the \benchBlocks{} reported dataset--length blocks, \benchSeries{} channel series were reconstructed by every combination without decoder failure.

On the floating-point matrix, the geometric decoder on the signed RP, GASF, GADF and XIRP recover the series to machine precision: the median NMAE per block never exceeds \floatMaxRP{} for the signed RP and \floatMaxGASF, \floatMaxGADF{} and \floatMaxXIRP{} for GASF, GADF and XIRP. The one exception is an ESC-50 window that is constant except for two samples, with an amplitude of about $3\times10^{-9}$. For these four, the 8-bit error is therefore storage error. It stays between \rpEightMin{} and \rpEightMax{} for the signed RP on six datasets and rises to \rpEightSwarmMin--\rpEightSwarmMax{} on Swarm; GASF and GADF are of the same order, and XIRP is about an order of magnitude lower. Sipers' reconstruction on the circular signed RP comes close, between \sipEightMin{} and \sipEightMax{}. Without a node threshold it is exact in floating point but less accurate after storage; with the threshold used here its floating-point error reaches a median of \floatMaxSIPERS{} per block (Supplementary Section~S5).

The remaining combinations reach an NMAE between about $0.03$ and $0.34$, for two different reasons (Figure~\ref{fig:examples}; spectral and correlational metrics in Supplementary Table~S7). On the unsigned RP, the geometric and Sipers' decoders keep the spectrum as well as on the signed RP, because a reflection leaves the spectrum unchanged; its pointwise error and its correlation of about zero come from returning the reflection in about half of the series. Hirata's reconstruction and the MTF representative also distort the spectrum and the energy: they lose the waveform, not only its sign.

\subsection{Recurrence plots: encoding versus decoder (RQ2)}\label{sec:results_rp}
Table~\ref{tab:fidelity} separates five effects. \emph{Thresholding.} On the floating-point binary RP, with the oracle orientation, neither decoder brings the error below \binaryOracleMin{} (up to \binaryOracleMax{} across datasets and lengths), and the two decoders stay within \binaryGapMax{} of each other: once the image is binary, most of the loss comes from the encoding, not from the algorithm. \emph{Sign.} On the unsigned RP, the geometric decoder with oracle orientation reaches the error of the signed RP, so its loss there is entirely a loss of orientation, which the sign restores from the image alone. \emph{Continuous distances.} On the same signed RP, the geometric decoder, which uses the distances that thresholding discards, is one to two orders of magnitude more accurate than Hirata's on six datasets; on Swarm the gap narrows to a factor of about two. \emph{Decoder on continuous distances.} Sipers' reconstruction also uses the continuous distances; with the sign it is a median of \macroDiffRPSIPERSSIGNED{} per series above the geometric decoder, which on the same circular image is \pilotRatioMin{} to \pilotRatioMax{} times more accurate on every dataset (Supplementary Table~S5). It is ahead in the benchmark only on Swarm, and the gain comes from the encoding: adding the wrap state raises the median $\sigma_2/\sigma_1$ from \swRatioNonCirc{} to \swRatioCirc{} and lowers the geometric decoder's error from \swGeoNonCirc{} to \swGeoCirc{}, below Sipers' \swSipCirc{} on the same image, while on other datasets it changes by at most \encGapMax{}. \emph{Threshold choice.} These comparisons do not depend on the recurrence rate fixed for Hirata: choosing the best rate per dataset with knowledge of the result lowers its error by at most \hirataGainUns{} on the unsigned and \hirataGainSig{} on the signed RP, leaving it between \hirataBestMin{} and \hirataBestMax{} (Supplementary Table~S3).

\subsection{Signal characteristics and stability (RQ3)}\label{sec:results_conditions}
Remark~\ref{rem:conditioning} predicts that storage error is amplified when the states are nearly collinear, and the data agree. None of the \condGoodSeries{} series with $\sigma_2/\sigma_1\ge0.1$ exceeds an 8-bit NMAE of $0.01$, whereas \condWorstRate\% of the series with $\sigma_2/\sigma_1<0.01$ do. The ratio is an empirical indicator, not a complete explanation: it separates the regimes at the two ends but does not determine which series in between fail. Nor does the share of sign-informative pairs: slow series are informative in nearly every pair, yet their states are the most collinear. Swarm, sampled at 1\,Hz, is the limiting case and the only dataset where the signed RP is not in the low-error group.

The sampling rate changes how far apart consecutive samples are, and with it the conditioning (Figure~\ref{fig:robustness_sampling}b--c). On PTB-XL with windows of 128 samples, from 50 to 500\,Hz the median $\sigma_2/\sigma_1$ falls from \srColFifty{} to \srColFivehundred{}, the share of series below $0.1$ grows from \srShareFifty\% to \srShareFivehundred\%, and the 8-bit NMAE of the signed RP rises from \srRPFifty{} to \srRPFivehundred{}, while GASF and GADF stay between \srGafMin{} and \srGafMax{} and XIRP is unaffected. A fixed number of samples spans a shorter time at a higher rate, but the trend persists at a fixed duration of 1.28\,s, from \srDurRPFifty{} to \srDurRPFivehundred{} (Supplementary Table~S4). Sipers' reconstruction rises with the rate as well (Figure~\ref{fig:robustness_sampling}b), so the effect belongs to the RP, not to one decoder. The resampled and the distributed 100\,Hz files give the same error (\srResampledRP{} and \srNativeRP{}). Resampling also changes the spectral content of each window, and the study covers one dataset, so the trend is attributable to the rate as realised by anti-aliased resampling of PTB-XL, not to the rate in isolation.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What each representation retains}
Three groups emerge, consistent across domains, lengths and classifiers. The signed RP with the geometric decoder, GASF, GADF on its restricted range and XIRP are exact in floating point under the stated conditions, so their error after storage is storage error, too small to affect classifiers trained on original signals. The unsigned RP determines the shape of the series but not its sign. The binary RP and MTF are many-to-one, so no decoder can be exact for every series, and the evaluated decoders lose most of the waveform; better approximations, for instance with learned priors, are not ruled out, but they cannot be read back with a guarantee of matching the encoded series.

\paragraph{What the sign and the decoder add}
RP reconstruction needs both. The sign fixes the orientation that the unsigned RP leaves open, and one sign-informative pair suffices (Proposition~\ref{prop:rp}). The continuous distances that thresholding discards account for the advantage over Hirata's reconstruction, and among decoders that use them the geometric one is more accurate and more robust than Sipers' on the same image, without assuming a periodic signal or choosing a frequency threshold.

\paragraph{When recovery becomes unstable}
The signed RP is the only exactly decodable representation whose stability depends strongly on the signal: slowly varying windows, from Swarm at 1\,Hz or from an ECG at 500\,Hz, have nearly collinear states that amplify storage error, for Sipers' decoder as for ours.

\paragraph{Using the benchmark}
The comparison is not a ranking, since a model may need a particular image. With recurrence plots, the signed RP and the geometric decoder give a route back to the series, with sufficient conditions for exactness, a measurable indicator of stability and less sensitivity to pixel noise than the angular fields on most datasets. GADF can be decoded if its angular range is restricted at encoding time; MTF and the binary RP give only approximations. Where the image is free to choose and the signal is sampled far above its bandwidth, the angular fields and XIRP are cheaper and independent of its conditioning. XIRP is an explicitly invertible reference: the redundancy of its column proposals plausibly explains its lower error, which says nothing about whether its images are better inputs for classification or generation.
\paragraph{Limitations}
Images are deterministic outputs of known encoders; pixel noise measures local stability only. Physical units need the per-channel calibration, which a generative setting would have to supply; only 8-bit storage and independent channels are studied. PTB-XL and ESC-50 are split by patient and recording, not by official folds. Hirata's reconstruction is our implementation, with a threshold the method does not prescribe, and Sipers' runs on the circular RP it requires, with a node threshold chosen in a pilot. The sampling-rate study covers one dataset, and $\sigma_2/\sigma_1$ is an empirical indicator, not an error bound.

\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusions}
We measured, under one framework, how much of a time series survives its image for the most widely used image representations and XIRP, on seven datasets from six domains. Four are exactly decodable in floating point under stated conditions and, after 8-bit storage, keep what classifiers trained on original signals need: the signed recurrence plot with a geometric decoder, GASF on $[0,1]$, GADF on a restricted angular range, and XIRP. The unsigned recurrence plot is recovered only up to sign; the binary recurrence plot and the Markov transition field, which are many-to-one, lose most of the waveform with the decoders evaluated. For recurrence plots, exact recovery needs the sign and a decoder that uses the continuous distances; stability after storage depends on how collinear the states are, and hence on the sampling rate. Images produced by generative models are the next step.
