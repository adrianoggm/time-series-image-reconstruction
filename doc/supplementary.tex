Figures~\ref{fig:INVRP} and~\ref{fig:INVGASF} show that RP and GASF distinguish this window from its sign-inverted counterpart. The same holds for GADF (Figure~\ref{fig:INVGADF}): under the symmetric normalisation used here, negating the input flips the sign of $\sin(\phi_i-\phi_j)$ and therefore reverses the image contrast. Conversely, the MTF images in Figure~\ref{fig:INVMTF} are identical. Under the ideal quantile relabelling $q\mapsto Q{+}1{-}q$, a global sign flip reverses both the bin order and the corresponding rows and columns of $W$, and these two permutations cancel in $M_{a,b}=W_{q_a,q_b}$. This operator-level sanity check illustrates the global-reversal ambiguity stated in Section~\ref{sec:mtf_reconstruction_method}; it does not by itself evaluate reconstruction fidelity, and tied quantiles can require additional implementation conventions.

\section{Additional qualitative diagnostics}
\subsection{Analytical control signal}
We construct an analytical three-channel signal \( \mathbf{w} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times 3} \) of length \(N=128\) by sampling an underlying parameter \(t\) uniformly over \([0,\pi]\). Concretely, define
\[
t_i = \frac{i}{N-1}\,\pi,\qquad i=0,1,\dots,N-1,
\]
and let each vector \(\mathbf{w}_i = [w_{i,0},\,w_{i,1},\,w_{i,2}]^\top\) be given by
\[
w_{i,0} = \cos(t_i),
\qquad
w_{i,1} = \sin(t_i),
\qquad
w_{i,2} = \sin(t_i)\;-\;\cos(t_i).
\]
Thus the full data matrix is
\[
\mathbf{w} =
\begin{bmatrix}
\cos(t_0) & \sin(t_0) & \sin(t_0)-\cos(t_0) \\
\cos(t_1) & \sin(t_1) & \sin(t_1)-\cos(t_1) \\
\vdots    & \vdots    & \vdots             \\
\cos(t_{N-1}) & \sin(t_{N-1}) & \sin(t_{N-1})-\cos(t_{N-1})
\end{bmatrix}.
\]
This signal, composed of a pure cosine channel, a pure sine channel, and their difference, serves as a controlled sanity check for the RP, GASF, GADF, and MTF reconstruction paths. It is not intended to represent the WISDM distribution or the output of a generative model; it provides a visually interpretable case where phase, shape, and orientation errors are easy to detect. Figure~\ref{fig:artificial_encodings} shows the image encodings of the three channels (RP is $127\times127$ because of the $N-1$ pair embedding; the others are $128\times128$).

\section{Robustness details and error attribution}
\label{sec:supp_robustness}

The local-stability sample is selected deterministically from window-level fold~0 (seed 0): 30 windows from each of the five activities, using round-robin sampling over the available subjects within each activity. The resulting 150 windows cover all 51 subjects. Fold~0 is only a reproducible sampling frame here: the decoders are untrained and no configuration is selected on this sample. For every nonzero noise level, two independent arrays are sampled over all pixels and channels, clipped to $[0,255]$, rounded to the 8-bit grid, and decoded; replicate errors are averaged within each window. These two draws reduce replicate-specific variation but are not sufficient to characterise the full noise distribution at an individual window. Accordingly, the reported standard deviations and intervals summarise variability \emph{across} windows, and the number of draws is exposed as \texttt{TFG\_ROBUST\_REPS} so the curve can be recomputed with more realizations. The subject-cluster intervals in Table~\ref{tab:supp_robustness_ci} resample the 51 represented subjects as whole clusters. The MTF encoder emitted tied-quantile warnings for some axis-series; allowing fewer distinct bins in those cases is part of the evaluated quantisation behaviour.

For the signed RP the cycle residual must not be read as a reconstruction-error measure. The Lu--Tong sign $\sigma(i,j)$ is a threshold on $\hat u_{ij}\cdot v$ at $\cos(\pi/4)$, so a pair whose displacement direction lies on that boundary flips sign under a perturbation of the order of double-precision rounding, and its entry then changes by $2|R_{ij}|$. Decoding the three axes of three windows directly from the native float operator reproduces the source signal to $\max_i|x_i-\hat x_i|\le7\times10^{-15}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, and yet the per-channel cycle residual ranges from exactly $0$, for channels in which no pair flips, to $0.036$. The RP row of Table~\ref{tab:supp_cycle_gate} is therefore dominated by boundary flips, and it is not comparable with the GASF, GADF and MTF rows, whose operators are continuous in the signal.

The per-source error decomposition (native float operator, clean 8-bit PNG, and perturbed PNG at $\sigma=8$) over these 150 windows is reported in the main text, Table~\ref{tab:error_sources}. It shows that RP, GASF, and GADF are all exact in floating point, so their clean error is entirely 8-bit quantisation, whereas MTF is already lossy before storage. RP's earlier residual float term came from the angular orientation grid and disappears with the closed-form orientation (Section~\ref{sec:supp_rp_orientation}).

\section{Window-level vs subject-out accuracy}
\label{sec:supp_window_vs_subject}

Absolute accuracy is higher under the window-level split, as expected when windows of a test subject may also appear in training, but the encoder ranking is identical under both protocols: RP, GASF and GADF track the original signal, while MTF degrades every classifier. \section{Subject-level classification equivalence}
\label{sec:supp_equivalence}

\paragraph*{Simultaneous max-deviation bootstrap algorithm.}
The central equivalence result is a joint subject-cluster bootstrap. Because it is the headline statistic, we state the exact procedure. Let a \emph{family} be a set of $C$ comparisons (here $C=18$ for the primary RP/GASF/GADF family, $C=6$ for the separate MTF control family), each a fixed (classifier, encoder) pair, evaluated over the same $K=51$ subjects in a fixed order.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Per-subject differences.} For subject $s$ and comparison $c$, compute the per-subject accuracy difference $\Delta_{c,s}=\mathrm{acc}^{\mathrm{orig}}_{s}-\mathrm{acc}^{\mathrm{recon}(c)}_{s}$, where each subject accuracy is that subject's fraction of correctly classified held-out windows (under subject-out validation every subject is a test subject in exactly one outer fold). This gives a $K\times C$ matrix with one row per subject.
\item \textbf{Point estimates.} The estimate for comparison $c$ is the subject mean $\hat\mu_c=\frac1K\sum_s\Delta_{c,s}$ (the ``mean $\Delta$acc'' column). Subjects, not windows, are the sampling unit; no per-subject weighting or standardisation is applied.
\item \textbf{Joint resampling.} For $b=1,\dots,B$ ($B=10^4$) draw one index vector of $K$ subjects with replacement and apply the \emph{same} vector to every column, so whole subjects are resampled jointly and the dependence between classifiers and encodings is preserved. Compute the resampled means $\hat\mu^{(b)}_c$ for all $c$.
\item \textbf{Family-wise critical value.} Centre each resampled mean on its point estimate, $d^{(b)}_c=\hat\mu^{(b)}_c-\hat\mu_c$ (raw accuracy units; the deviations are \emph{not} studentised or divided by a bootstrap standard error), take the family maximum $D^{(b)}=\max_c|d^{(b)}_c|$, and set the single critical value $\kappa$ to the $95$th percentile of $\{D^{(b)}\}_{b=1}^{B}$. One $\kappa$ is shared by all comparisons in the family, which is what makes the coverage simultaneous rather than pointwise.
\item \textbf{Intervals and decision.} The simultaneous interval for comparison $c$ is $[\hat\mu_c-\kappa,\;\hat\mu_c+\kappa]$. Equivalence at margin $m$ is declared iff that whole interval lies inside $[-m,+m]$; the main text uses $m=0.01$ with $m=0.02$ as a less stringent sensitivity check.
\end{enumerate}
Each family uses an independent random seed and its own $\kappa$; no resamples or critical values are shared between the primary and MTF families, and none are pooled across the subject-out and window-level protocols. The compact main-text table reports, for each method, the single classifier-specific interval whose endpoint lies closest to the equivalence margin. The wider ``interval spans'' used later in Table~\ref{tab:supp_equivalence_protocols} are unions of the classifier-specific intervals and are descriptive only. This is the \texttt{\_cluster\_bootstrap\_simultaneous} routine in \texttt{scripts/\_stat\_tests.py}; the confirmatory paired TOST uses the same per-subject $\Delta_{c,s}$ but a Student-$t$ interval at the bilateral $90\%$ level, so the simultaneous $95\%$ bootstrap is the stricter headline rule.

Table~\ref{tab:supp_equivalence_individual} exposes all classifier-specific simultaneous intervals behind the compact worst-case summary in the main article. The primary family contains the 18 RP/GASF/GADF comparisons; MTF's six negative-control comparisons form a separate family. Within each family, all classifiers share the same $10^4$ subject resamples and the same bootstrap maximum-deviation critical value. Thus these are simultaneous intervals, not six unadjusted pointwise intervals and not an ``envelope CI.''

\section{RP orientation: angular grid versus closed form}
\label{sec:supp_rp_orientation}

The orientation step of Section~\ref{sec:rp_reconstruction_method} was previously a search over a grid of $n_\theta=720$ rotation angles crossed with the two reflections, keeping the eight lowest-overlap candidates for the signed comparison. It is now solved exactly: for each reflection parity the translation-corrected overlap residual is linear in $(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)$, so the minimiser is an eigenvector of a symmetric $2\times2$ matrix, giving four exact candidates. Table~\ref{tab:supp_rp_orientation} compares the two on the same 150 activity-balanced windows (450 axis-series) used by the robustness block, with the metadata-free inversion in both arms so that only the orientation differs.

Three points follow. First, on the native float operator the closed form is exact to double precision (mean NMAE $9.1\times10^{-17}$; mean normalised squared-overlap diagnostic $3.2\times10^{-31}$), whereas the grid retains $3.5\times10^{-4}$ --- the discretisation error of a $0.5^\circ$ spacing. This is the term the main text previously attributed to the RP algorithm. Second, the advantage survives 8-bit storage (0.00101 versus 0.00120) but is progressively masked by pixel noise, the two arms differing by under $3\times10^{-4}$ from $\sigma=2$ onwards. Third, total decoding takes 0.27 versus 0.60\,s per axis-series in this finite-$N$ Python implementation. Although MDS remains the asymptotically dominant $O(N^3)$ step, at $N=127$ the interpreted loop over 1,440 grid/reflection candidates dominates wall-clock time; the closed form removes that loop.

The cycle residual is 0.0533 against 0.0512 on clean PNG and 0.1627 against 0.1596 at $\sigma=8$ (closed form versus grid). These differences do not measure reconstruction quality: as shown in Section~\ref{sec:supp_robustness}, the signed-RP cycle residual is governed by sign flips at the $\cos(\pi/4)$ threshold, which a float-exact reconstruction also produces. The column is reported for completeness and no comparison is drawn from it.

The last column counts axis-series where the signed-RP criterion did not select the candidate with the lowest NMAE against the ground truth. The closed form never mis-selects on clean input, float or 8-bit. The grid's counts are large but not comparable in kind: its eight-candidate shortlist contains near-identical angles, so a different pick costs a mean NMAE regret of only $2.0\times10^{-4}$ on clean 8-bit input rather than a wrong reflection or sign.

\section{GADF storage range and pivot sensitivity}

\paragraph*{Theoretical operator range and fixed PNG map.}
Under the restricted normalisation $\bar x_i\in[-\alpha,\alpha]$, the largest possible angular separation is $\pi-2\arccos(\alpha)$. The corresponding GADF magnitude is therefore bounded by
\[
\max_{i,j}|G^{\mathrm D}_{ij}|
=\sin\!\left(\pi-2\arccos\alpha\right)
=2\alpha\sqrt{1-\alpha^2}.
\]
For $\alpha=0.7$, this bound is approximately $0.9998$, and every non-constant window attains it, because the restricted normalisation places one sample at $-\alpha$ and another at $+\alpha$. The encoder's per-window min--max map is therefore exactly the fixed map $[-0.9998,0.9998]\to[0,255]$, whereas the decoder inverts the image with $[-1,1]$ and so applies a deterministic gain of $1.00013$. The resulting discrepancy is about $2\times10^{-4}$ at either endpoint, below one 8-bit intensity step. This is a deterministic consequence of the declared branch restriction, not an empirical estimate, and is why no per-window range metadata is needed.

\paragraph*{Pivot-ensemble size.}
Averaging the reconstruction over the $n_p$ most-central rows, rather than using a single pivot, is the second numerical choice. Table~\ref{tab:supp_gadf_npivots} reports a descriptive sweep on 1,500 windows drawn without replacement from the complete WISDM window set with seed 0. On this sample, pointwise error falls monotonically from a single pivot ($0.0353$) to five ($0.0158$, the fixed headline setting) and continues to $0.0119$ at $n_p=9$. The measured wall-clock differences are small because each additional pivot adds an $O(N)$ pass over an already-loaded matrix; these timings describe this implementation run and are not intended as a portable hardware benchmark. The sweep shows the effect of averaging but is not used to revise the already fixed headline configuration.

\section{RP--GADF structural diagnostic}
\subsection{Null-Corrected RP/GADF Regional Overlap}
\label{sec:disc_rp_gadf_overlap}

The visual proximity between RP and GADF is easy to overstate from a single example, so we add a diagnostic test that deliberately separates spatial region alignment from marginal colour statistics. On the full set of non-overlapping smartwatch-accelerometer WISDM windows (window length \(N=128\)), we encode the same signal as signed RP and GADF, align the matrices on the common \(127\times127\) support, and quantise each image channel into \(B\) visual intensity ranges. For every matched pair \((\mathrm{RP}_i,\mathrm{GADF}_i)\), we compare the quantised regions against a conservative null pair \((\mathrm{RP}_i,\mathrm{GADF}_j)\), where \(j\neq i\) is drawn from the same activity class. The primary statistic is a weighted regional Jaccard score, \(\sum_b |R_b\cap G_b|/\sum_b |R_b\cup G_b|\), averaged across the grayscale view and the three RGB/axis channels. We also compute a distribution-only overlap that ignores pixel position and a boundary-F1 score between quantised region edges.

\subsection{Structural relationship between signed RP and GADF}
\label{sec:supp_structural}
\paragraph*{Operator-level comparison of pairwise structure.}
Before discussing the first-order amplitude expansion, it is useful to put signed RP and GADF in a common notation without claiming equality of their output spaces. Both can be written as pairwise functions of two-dimensional states,
\[
M_{ij} \;=\; \omega(z_i,z_j),
\]
where $z_i\in\mathbb{R}^{2}$ is method specific and $\omega:\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}$ is the associated pairwise operator. The two methods differ in both the state construction and the operator:
\begin{itemize}
  \item RP under the Lu--Tong convention uses the sequence-dependent adjacent state \(z_k=(x_k,\,x_{k+1})\) and a signed Euclidean distance, \(\omega_{\text{RP}}(u,v)=\sigma(u,v)\lVert u-v\rVert_{2}\), where the sign is assigned \emph{externally} by the projection onto the reference direction \(v_{\star}=(1,1)^{\top}/\sqrt{2}\). With the thresholded sign rule used here, \(\sigma(u,v)\) is not guaranteed to satisfy \(\sigma(u,v)=-\sigma(v,u)\) for every pair, so the resulting matrix is directional but not necessarily antisymmetric.
  \item GADF uses the polar state \(z_k=(\tilde x_k,\sqrt{1-\tilde x_k^{2}})=(\cos\phi_k,\sin\phi_k)\) and the operator \(\omega_{\text{GADF}}(u,v)=\det[v\mid u]=u_{2}v_{1}-u_{1}v_{2}=\sin(\phi_i-\phi_j)\), the signed area of the parallelogram spanned by the two states, whose sign is \emph{intrinsic} to the operator and matches \(G^{\mathrm{D}}_{ij}\) in Eq.~\eqref{eq:gadf_indexed}.
\end{itemize}
The important consequence is therefore an analogy, not an identity. GADF lies \emph{within} the $\binom{N}{2}$-dimensional space of antisymmetric zero-diagonal matrices by construction, because the determinant changes sign when its arguments are swapped; more specifically, its factorisation $G^{\mathrm{D}}=\mathbf{s}\,\mathbf{c}^{\top}-\mathbf{c}\,\mathbf{s}^{\top}$ (with $s_i=\sin\phi_i$, $c_i=\cos\phi_i$) shows it occupies only a rank-$\leq 2$ subvariety of that ambient space, not the whole of it. Signed RP has a zero diagonal and carries directional pairwise-distance information, but the Lu--Tong sign rule used in this paper does not force exact antisymmetry for nearly orthogonal displacements. The first-order expansion below quantifies why the two images can nevertheless look and behave similarly, while the structural differences between the two operators --- GADF's intrinsic sign and its closed-form one-pass inverse, versus signed RP's externally assigned sign and its MDS-based reconstruction --- follow from the determinant-based operator rather than from an exact shared matrix space.

\paragraph*{Local first-order analogy.}
The visual proximity between RP and GADF can be examined through a local approximation without claiming a shared operator. GADF produces an antisymmetric zero-diagonal matrix, while signed RP produces a zero-diagonal directional distance matrix with related pairwise-difference content. For smooth signals whose local increments vary slowly (so that $x_{i+1}-x_i\approx x_{j+1}-x_j$ over the compared indices), both can be approximated by functions of the pairwise-difference matrix $\{x_i-x_j\}_{i,j}$. The signed RP under the Lu--Tong embedding satisfies
\[
R_{ij} \;\approx\; \sigma(i,j)\,\sqrt{2}\,|x_i-x_j|,
\]
where $\sigma(i,j)$ is the external orientation flag computed from the projection of $s_i-s_j$ onto $v=(1,1)^\top/\sqrt{2}$. The GADF entry, expanded around a common normalized amplitude $\bar{x}$, satisfies
\[
G^{\mathrm{D}}_{ij} \;=\; \sin(\phi_i-\phi_j) \;\approx\; \frac{\bar{x}_j-\bar{x}_i}{\sqrt{1-\bar{x}^2}},
\]
because $d(\arccos x)/dx=-1/\sqrt{1-x^2}$, with both the samples $\bar{x}_i,\bar{x}_j$ and the expansion point $\bar{x}$ taken in the restricted $[-\alpha,\alpha]$ normalization of the GADF decoder. The sign of the GADF term is intrinsic to the trigonometric form rather than imposed externally. Thus, both expressions depend on pairwise amplitude differences at first order and both are zero on the diagonal, but only GADF is strictly antisymmetric under the stated construction. The restricted-branch GADF inverse is closed form because the angular interval $[\arccos(\alpha),\arccos(-\alpha)]$, with $\alpha=0.7<1/\sqrt2$, keeps every angular difference on the principal branch and the image-derived anchor restores the global offset. Signed RP instead recovers a two-dimensional state geometry through MDS and uses overlap plus its external sign convention to choose an orientation. The approximation offers a plausible local explanation for visual similarity; it does not imply that the matrices contain identical information or admit the same inverse.

\paragraph*{Practical interpretation.}
The operator analysis and the regional diagnostic are consistent with the empirical finding that both RP and GADF preserve enough directional pairwise structure for accurate controlled reconstruction, but they reach that result through different contracts. GASF supplies single-sample information on its diagonal; GADF supplies anchored angular differences under a restricted branch; and signed RP supplies adjacent-state distances plus an external orientation convention. The fidelity and equivalence results establish the usefulness of all three under those declared contracts. They do not establish a formal information equivalence among the encodings or a general preference outside the controlled reconstruction task.
